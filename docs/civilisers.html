01

The resemblance is real. Its shape matters.

Which work is new? This is V2: the source-audit pilot, not a new run of the original similarity experiment. It assembles 29 passages for inspection. A stronger comparison study is proposed below; it has not been run.

A figure who brings knowledge and disappears over the sea is a compelling story. But a visitor from the sea, a creator who leaves by sea and a being who returns there every night are three different stories.

The first experiment found that AI judges often preferred the proposed civiliser pairs to the decoys provided. It did not establish how rare those parallels are among other teaching figures, or where they came from. Here the unit is a passage, not an AI-compressed biography.

29 passages · 10 source records · 10 figure entries
A deliberately selected reading set, not independent trials or a worldwide sample.

Knowledge, then a remarkable departure: a parallel survives.

Sahagún's account attributes crafts to Quetzalcoatl and ends his journey with a raft made of serpents. Sarmiento's Viracocha instructs people, then walks over the sea with two servants. Instruction followed by an extraordinary sea departure is a genuine correspondence in these accounts—even though the teaching, companions and means of travel differ.

The crafts · Q1 / The serpent raft · Q3 / Walking over water · V3

The sea belongs at different ends of the story.

Oannes explicitly emerges from the sea and returns there at night. Quetzalcoatl and Viracocha leave over it in the inspected endings. Piedrahíta's Bochica dies at Sogamoso and ascends to heaven; the road associated with his coming is inland. A single label, “came from the sea,” would erase much of what we need to explain.

Oannes arrives · O1 / His nightly routine · O3 / Quetzalcoatl leaves · Q3 / Viracocha leaves · V3 / Bochica's ending · B3

“After the flood” does not describe all four.

Oannes appears before the later flood assigned to Xisuthrus. Viracocha is a creator who destroys a population before the subsequent creation. Bochica opens a way for floodwater to drain. These are striking relationships to catastrophe, but not the same episode of a knowledgeable survivor arriving afterward.

Before the flood · O4 / Destruction and creation · V1 / The next creation · V2 / Flood relief · B2

A future messenger is not quite a promise to return.

Sarmiento's Viracocha promises messengers. The closing Quetzalcoatl paragraph inspected here gives no personal return promise; that is a statement about this paragraph, not every Quetzalcoatl tradition. Brinton's 1882 comparative introduction does make future reappearance part of its general hero formula. This gives us a later synthesis to trace—not a date when the belief was invented.

The messengers · V4 / The closing paragraph · Q3 / The later formula · R2

The best comparisons also bring knowledge.

Prometheus claims writing, numbers, buildings, ships, medicine and more: a substantial alternative comparison for Oannes' catalogue of arts. In the Sumerian story of Inana and Enki, crafts travel between cities as divine powers loaded onto a boat. That is not direct teaching by a sea visitor, but it is an informative local analogue. Neither is assumed to be historically unrelated.

Oannes' curriculum · O2 / Prometheus' arts · P1 / Medicine and metals · P2 / The divine powers · I1 / The boat · I2

The chroniclers were already looking for a connected world.

Sarmiento frames American origins through Atlantis and Mesopotamia. Piedrahíta proposes Saint Bartholomew as an explanation for Bochica. These interpretations belong to the recording authors, not automatically to the people whose traditions they report. Their presence makes source history essential; it does not show that all the underlying stories were invented.

Sarmiento's framework · V5 / Piedrahíta's interpretation · B4

A summary can quietly combine the teachers, too.

In the inspected Garcilaso excerpt, Manco Cápac is associated with cultivation and waterworks, while Mama Ocllo teaches textile work. Compressing their contribution into one male civiliser would manufacture a broader individual curriculum. Keeping the actors separate preserves a richer story as well as a fairer comparison.

04

The witnesses behind the words

The age of a surviving record, the date of its modern edition and the unknown age of a tradition are different things. A chain of translations is also not a series of independent confirmations. These records keep those distinctions visible.

berossus · Mediated ancient account

Eusebius, Chronicle, Book I: Berossus fragments

Composition / record
Berossus wrote in the early third century BCE; Eusebius compiled his Chronicle in the fourth century CE. Neither date is a date for Oannes' supposed life.
Witness and edition inspected
Robert Bedrosian's 2008 English translation of the Armenian version; sections [4]–[7] inspected.
Source family / mediation
Berossus → excerpting authors including Alexander Polyhistor → Eusebius → Armenian transmission → English. These are links in a chain, not independent confirmations.
Still to check
Compare Greek fragments and specialist editions; do not treat the complete original Babyloniaca as surviving.
sarmiento · Colonial account

Pedro Sarmiento de Gamboa, History of the Incas

Composition / record
1572 account, commissioned in the administration of Viceroy Toledo.
Witness and edition inspected
Clements R. Markham's 1907 English translation, in the Project Gutenberg transcription; chapters V–VII inspected.
Source family / mediation
An account recorded under colonial rule, with the author's own biblical and classical interpretation. Markham's footnotes are kept separate from Sarmiento's narrative.
Still to check
Check against the Spanish manuscript and earlier Betanzos and Cieza accounts; this is an inspected witness, not a claim of earliest attestation.
sahagun · Colonial collaborative account

Sahagún and Nahua collaborators, Historia general, Book III

Composition / record
Sixteenth-century compilation; the Florentine Codex was completed in 1577.
Witness and edition inspected
Spanish transcription in the linked PDF. Chapters III, IV and XII–XIV inspected; the transcription's edition provenance is not supplied clearly enough to assign an edition year.
Source family / mediation
Nahua knowledge recorded through a Franciscan project. This audit reads Spanish, not an independent Nahuatl translation. English explanations are project paraphrases.
Still to check
Collate the transcription with the Getty Digital Florentine Codex and the Nahuatl column. Consult the Anales de Cuauhtitlan as a separate version.
piedrahita · Colonial account

Lucas Fernández de Piedrahíta, Historia general de las conquistas del Nuevo Reyno de Granada

Composition / record
Published 1688; this is not a pre-contact record.
Witness and edition inspected
Digitised 1688 edition, Book I, chapter III, printed pp. 17–19 (PDF pp. 43–45).
Source family / mediation
A colonial chronicler reports traditions and supplies Christian comparisons. His report and his explanation must not become one unmarked narrative.
Still to check
Follow the account back into Pedro Simón and other earlier witnesses. Weaving and more specific directional claims require those passages, not a modern summary.
prometheus · Ancient literary account

Prometheus Bound, traditionally attributed to Aeschylus

Composition / record
Classical Greek drama; authorship and exact dating are not resolved by this audit.
Witness and edition inspected
Herbert Weir Smyth's English translation, 1926, as transcribed by Theoi; lines 228–256 and 436–506 inspected.
Source family / mediation
A literary speech about the origins of arts, not a first-person historical record. Included as a role comparator, not proof of an unrelated invention.
Still to check
Compare other Prometheus versions and more closely matched genres.
demeter · Ancient literary account

Homeric Hymn to Demeter

Composition / record
Archaic Greek hymn; the collection's dating is approximate and this audit does not date the oral tradition.
Witness and edition inspected
H. G. Evelyn-White, 1914; lines 250–313 and 470–489 inspected.
Source family / mediation
Anonymous hymn in English translation. A Greek comparator alongside Prometheus, not an independent statistical draw.
Still to check
Ritual teaching is explicit; do not substitute a different story about the invention of agriculture.
changes · Received textual tradition

Book of Changes, Great Treatise, second section

Composition / record
The received commentary's composition and manuscript history have not been dated in this audit. It is not a contemporary record of the legendary sovereigns.
Witness and edition inspected
James Legge's translation, The Yî King (1882), Appendix III, section II, chapter II, paragraphs 11–15, pp. 382–384.
Source family / mediation
Fuxi and Shennong are two figures in the same textual sequence. They count as one source family, not two independent witnesses.
Still to check
Collate the received Chinese text and early manuscripts; retain the distinction between commentary and the core divination text.
inana · Composite scholarly edition

Inana and Enki

Composition / record
Sumerian literary tradition; exact composition and individual tablet dates are outside this initial audit.
Witness and edition inspected
Oxford Electronic Text Corpus of Sumerian Literature, translation 1.3.1; segments D, F and H inspected.
Source family / mediation
A scholarly reconstruction with gaps and variant witnesses explicitly marked. A Mesopotamian local analogue to the cultural-arts theme, not an unrelated control.
Still to check
Consult the individual tablets and bibliography before making chronological or textual-independence claims.
garcilaso · Colonial account

Garcilaso de la Vega, Royal Commentaries: Manco Cápac and Mama Ocllo

Composition / record
Royal Commentaries, part I, first published 1609.
Witness and edition inspected
Excerpts from Paul Rycaut's 1688 translation, edited by Jared Aragona; Book I, chapters VII and XII in this edition's numbering.
Source family / mediation
Garcilaso's account → Rycaut → edited teaching excerpt. An Andean local comparator; not independent of all traditions associated with Viracocha.
Still to check
Compare the complete Spanish edition and other origin accounts. The excerpt is shorter and differently selected than the main cases.
brinton · Later comparative retelling

Daniel G. Brinton, American Hero-Myths

Composition / record
Published 1882.
Witness and edition inspected
Project Gutenberg transcription, introductory chapter's outline of the culture-hero narrative.
Source family / mediation
A comparative author's generalisation across traditions. Not an additional Indigenous witness or proof that each named figure has every listed trait.

05

What this can—and cannot—tell us

This is an exploratory audit, not a rarity test. The figures and passages were deliberately selected after seeing the original result. There is no worldwide sampling frame, no blinding, no independent specialist validation and no probability estimate. Ten source records do not mean ten independent traditions.

It is not yet a complete source genealogy. These are early accessible witnesses, not a claim to have found the earliest attestation of every detail. Earlier Andean and Colombian accounts, a Nahuatl comparison, alternative Quetzalcoatl versions and specialist editions of the Berossus fragments remain important gaps.

The sources are uneven. A drama, a colonial chronicle and a reconstructed Sumerian composition have different purposes. Some figures receive more reading than others. A detail missing from a short excerpt is especially weak negative evidence. Later records allow influence; their date alone does not demonstrate borrowing.

The positive result is a clearer comparison. Some specific parallels survive. Others separate into different events or different actors. We can now point to the passages that a proposed explanation must account for. We cannot use this set to establish a lost civilisation, exclude deep inheritance, or choose between transmission and independent invention.

A stronger redo: find the best competing matches

Proposed, not run. Ask whether these figures share a more distinctive story than other teachers and founders in a defined collection. Begin with the four named targets and roughly 20–30 plausible comparators, with comparable source coverage. The final collection, rubric and stopping rule still need to be fixed.

  1. Build source packets. Read complete relevant episodes, keep variants separate and record how much was inspected for every figure.
  2. Compare relationships as well as details. Preserve actors and event order while allowing a broader narrative parallel to survive literal differences. Require passage support on both sides.
  3. Show the strongest alternatives. Compare each target with every eligible candidate and publish its best matches, their differences and independent readers' disagreements.
  4. Check what survives. Try alternative source versions and set generic teaching/travel aside. Test the reading method on known paraphrases and clearly labelled artificial contrasts before trusting its rankings.

The first result should be an inspectable atlas of the best matches, with ranks limited to the actual collection—not a probability of a lost civilisation. Source-family dependence, uneven coverage and the AI's prior knowledge remain limitations. Read the proposed protocol and unresolved choices →
