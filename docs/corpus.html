Nothing selected. Both walls show every registered photograph.

Of 39 Anatolian photographs, a bird is legible in 8 and a rounded form in 4. Both share one surface in 3 — and those 3 photographs are 1 object. Of 33 Rapanui photographs the figures are 13, 8 and 3, across 2 objects.

That asymmetry is the result of the exercise. Birds are the dominant subject of the Rapanui carvings registered here and an occasional one among the Anatolian animals — and in this sample the Anatolian half of the comparison rests on a single stone, photographed three times. None of that decides whether the resemblance means anything. It does decide what the resemblance is between: not two bird-centred repertoires, but one pillar and a tradition.

Why the motif lives here

Each side has a local story.

Before a resemblance can cross an ocean it has to survive the question of why each side would produce it anyway. The two answers here are not symmetrical.

10th–9th millennia BCE · Pre-Pottery Neolithic

Neolithic Anatolia

The carved repertoire here is overwhelmingly animal: foxes, boars, aurochs, snakes, scorpions, spiders, leopards and cranes, arranged on and around T-shaped pillars that are themselves abstracted human bodies, with arms, hands and belts. Birds are one animal among many rather than the subject of the imagery, and no documented ritual explains the disc on Pillar 43. What the carvings meant is exactly what the excavators say remains open.

Pillar 43: of animals and a headless man · The excavation team’s own discussion of the animals and the competing readings of the disc.

Later carvings · second millennium CE

Rapanui traditions

Rapa Nui has a documented reason to carve birdmen holding rounded forms. At Orongo, an annual competition sent servants to swim to the islet of Motu Nui and return with the first egg of the manutara, the sooty tern; the sponsor of the winner became the tangata manu, the birdman, for that year. The densest birdman carvings cluster at Mata Ngarau, the ceremonial outcrop above that competition. A bird beside an egg is the subject of the ritual itself.

How this page was built

The rule,
before the looking.

Files were listed from fixed Wikimedia Commons categories for each tradition, before any of them were looked at. A file was kept if it is a photograph in which one carved or painted surface is the subject and legible enough to say what is and is not visible on it. Undecorated pillars were kept deliberately: they belong to the population the question is asked against.

Photographic coverage is not corpus coverage. The published corpora are far larger than what Commons holds — Göbekli Tepe’s excavated pillars are catalogued by the excavation team, and the Orongo petroglyphs were surveyed in the standard Rapa Nui rock-art corpus. Neither total is reproduced here, so no frequency on this page is a frequency in either tradition.

Feature states are source-informed editorial readings of each registered photograph, prepared with AI; independent review is outstanding. “Legible” means the feature can be seen in that photograph. “Not legible in this view” is not a finding that the object lacks the feature, and this page cannot be used to argue that anything is absent.

What was excluded, and why
  • Site views in which no single carved surface can be attributed96 files
  • Maps, plans, diagrams, transcriptions and modern drawings41 files
  • Museum rooms, signage, visitors and landscape without carving63 files
  • Later-period museum holdings outside the Neolithic sites370 files
  • Further photographs of surfaces already registered97 files
  • Dropped on inspection: rock with no legible carving, montages, annotated composites and one illustration19 files

Counts are of files listed, not of objects. Every photograph that survived the rule was then opened at full size and checked; eighteen were dropped at that stage, including a modern illustration of a bull, two annotated composites and eleven views of rock in which no carving could actually be read. The "further photographs" group is the honest reservoir: a second batch can register more surfaces without changing the rule.
