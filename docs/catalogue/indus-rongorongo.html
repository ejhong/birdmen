What makes it a question?

Selected sign charts propose affinities across a large gap in time and geography. A script relationship should predict more than a few convincing outlines.

The project audits the circulating comparison chart and historical attribution to de Hevesy; the original proposing publication still deserves a complete source review.

What differs or remains uncertain

  • The circulating chart and published inventories do not always depict the same forms.
  • The old metric’s retrieval validation was imperfect; its rankings cannot settle the human-visible comparison.
  • A corpus’s writing system, language and chronology are separate questions.
Transmission check

No route established in this review

No transmission sequence for this specific comparison has been established in the sources reviewed. That is not proof that contact was impossible.

What would move this forward

  • Compare untouched inscriptions as well as drawings.
  • Test recurring sign sequences and structural correspondences with specialists.
  • Maintain wood dates and engraving dates separately.

Sources & scope

Provisional editorial record; no independent specialist sign-off. Status describes review depth, not the probability of a historical connection.

  1. project · Deep Memory

    Indus and rongorongo: the comparison-chart audit

    Scanned sign inventories, the circulating chart and metric limitations. Recognition was imperfect; rankings do not settle historical relationship.

  2. museum · The Metropolitan Museum of Art

    Indus seal · 49.40.1

    Exact pictured seal and modern impression, dated ca. 2600–1900 BCE. This photograph is an example, not the inventory used for the old metric.

  3. research · Ferrara et al., 2024

    New radiocarbon dates on rongorongo

    Four tablets dated; one has earlier wood. Wood age and inscription age must be distinguished. This does not date every sign or settle an Indus connection.

  4. museum · British Museum

    Rongorongo tablet · Oc1903,-.150

    Specific object has a 1700–1850 catalogue range. The older account of script origins is not a settled chronology of the whole tradition.
