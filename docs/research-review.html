Research review / September 2026

What did the tests
actually test?

The question is larger than any one experiment. A review of the evidence, the instruments and the conclusions they can support.

The common problem

Validate the observer before trusting the verdict.

The old studies contain useful observations, but some conclusions outran the measurements.

The visual experiments sometimes failed to recognise features; the text experiments compressed or broadened the very details they were meant to compare. A numerical output can be reproducible while measuring the wrong thing.

The revised site treats each result as conditional on its inputs and method. Original prompts, responses and records remain intact. The following changes concern interpretation and the design of replacement studies.

Images / 03

The pillar and the moai.

Keep: the photographs, model disagreements, object-type effects and saved descriptions. Opus’s high relative ranking is part of the record and should not disappear into a summary that says every model found the pair ordinary.

Correct: the models were unlabelled, not demonstrably blind. The main scoring prompt asked whether a resemblance suggested a shared tradition, mixing observation and causal interpretation. Sub-scores from the same prompt are not an independent resemblance-only test. Matching object type changed several things at once; it did not validate the measurement.

Pilot A’s agreement on birds was about 0.31, on snakes 0.25 and on posture 0.20. Pilot C gave targets more pixels than candidates, reused views of the same objects and applied isolation unevenly. Its most restricted decoys came from only three other traditions. These conditions constrain both positive and negative claims.

A saved response, not a hypothetical objection
“B depicts a single blocky anthropomorphic figure with facial features and hands meeting at the belly”

The input was the back photograph of Hoa Hakananai’a. This response gave the pair 2/10. Another response described no identifiable animals; the source records identify birdman carvings. A 6/10 response also described long-fingered hands meeting at a belt on Pillar 43—an arrangement absent from that photographed surface. High scores need the same feature checks.

Four preserved target examples, full text and original file paths · Original qualifications. These examples were selected to demonstrate failures, not to estimate a failure rate.

Replacement: first inspect perception; then compare literal shapes and relationships, using equal image quality, object-level deduplication, local families and well-matched alternatives. Humans and models must demonstrate that they can read the scenes. The recognition diagnostic has now run; the historical comparison remains to be done.

Original results · Original protocol and amendments · Replacement design.

Signs / 01

Indus and rongorongo.

Keep: the catalogue comparison, illustration-fidelity checks and demonstration that rendering style changes rankings.

Correct: the claim that human eyes are the wrong instrument, or that a coarse metric settles visual resemblance. In the independent-rendition validation, the combined method’s top-one retrieval was 75.4%, compared with 80.5% for HOG alone; the visual embedding component was only 11.6%. Adding a feature does not automatically improve the instrument.

Replacement: validate on independently drawn versions of known signs and matched hard alternatives before comparing unrelated scripts. Include different catalogue conventions, actual inscriptions where legible, human judgments, and family-level features. Choose the metric on a development set and evaluate on held-out signs. A visual match remains distinct from shared sound, meaning or descent.

Study and limitations · Saved validation results.

Motifs / 02

Birds, serpents and sacred centres.

Keep: the finding that the selected broad categories are widespread and that documentation density affects their apparent bundling.

Correct: myths about becoming or marrying birds are not the same motif as a bird-headed figure beside a disc. A dispersed distribution is not a unique signature of independent invention. Thin coverage, especially in the 2016 catalogue’s ancient Near Eastern and Rapanui material, limits negative conclusions.

Replacement: define the claimed motif at several levels of specificity. Link every positive to an actual passage, preserve variants and record missing evidence as unknown. Compare transmission, inheritance and convergence explicitly rather than identifying a cause from a clustering statistic alone.

Catalogue experiment · Original category definitions.

Teaching figures / 04

The civilisers.

Keep: the striking 62.5% first-place result and the later source audit. Positive evidence deserves the same methodological attention as negative evidence.

Correct: removing names did not blind the accounts. In the recognition check Opus identified 96 of 103 regions. Modern summaries compress stories into familiar templates; some controls have different narrative roles, and variants within a tradition are not independent witnesses to worldwide transmission.

Replacement: use source-linked passages, comparable narrative roles and documented translation histories. Keep variants within a tradition grouped. Test similarity both before and after disclosing context. The existing V2 audit improves the evidence, but has not rerun V1’s experiment; the V3 draft sets out a possible redesign.

Original lineups · Passage-level source audit · V3 design, not yet run.

Flood stories / 05

The raven and the dove.

Keep: regional variation in the coded summaries and the opportunity to inspect specific story details.

Correct: one model coded a compiler’s summaries. Generic warnings and animals are not highly specific evidence of a Genesis source. Post-contact recording is not the same as post-contact invention, and regional structure does not exclude older inheritance.

Replacement: trace each account to its earliest available record; separate date of recording, possible borrowing and actual evidence of textual dependence. Independently code rare sequences—such as which birds are sent, in which order and with what result—alongside generic features. Evaluate alternative codings and uncertainty without treating every summary as an equally independent witness.

The original pilot and its qualifications.

A better research sequence

Source → observe → compare → explain.

The new central dossier and recognition test implement the first steps. The rest requires broader source collection and independent review before another large batch of AI judgments would be informative.

The archive’s software tests now check links, source integrity, unchanged photographs, honest reporting of missing answers, and whether failed recognition keeps historical ranking disabled. Passing software tests says the site works; it does not certify an archaeological claim.
