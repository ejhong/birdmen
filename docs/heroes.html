Study 4: are the civilisers one story?

Viracocha walked out of Lake Titicaca and taught the Andes to farm. Quetzalcoatl brought maize and the calendar to Mexico and left by sea, promising to return. Oannes came out of the Persian Gulf with the body of a fish and taught Sumer writing and law. Osiris civilised Egypt and was murdered by his brother. The lost-civilisation books put these side by side and ask how they can be so alike. This page asks the same question of a machine that cannot see the names: are these stories more alike than culture-hero stories ordinarily are, and are they as alike as stories that are known to share a source?

02

The claim

The argument goes back at least to Daniel Brinton's American Hero-Myths (1882), which set Quetzalcoatl, Viracocha, Itzamna and Michabo side by side, and it is central to Graham Hancock's Fingerprints of the Gods: bearded, pale strangers arrive by sea after a catastrophe, teach agriculture, law, building, writing and the calendar, are gentle, forbid sacrifice, are driven out or murdered, and leave promising to return. Hancock reads the agreement as memory of the same people, survivors of a drowned civilisation, remembered in Peru as Viracocha, in Mexico as Quetzalcoatl and Kukulkan, in Colombia as Bochica, in Sumer as Oannes and in Egypt as Osiris. The scholarly reading is that each figure is at home in its own tradition, that the "white and bearded" details in the American cases appear in Spanish colonial sources and served Spanish purposes, and that culture heroes who bring fire, crops and law are among the most widespread figures in world mythology because every society has a story about where its arts came from.

That last point is the test. If civiliser myths are alike everywhere, the Hancock pairs should be no more alike than any two culture-hero stories from unrelated places. If the Hancock pairs share something specific, a judge who cannot see the names should pick each out from a lineup of nine strangers, the way it can pick Genesis out of a lineup when shown Gilgamesh.

03

Blinding the stories

Each myth is the narrative of one English Wikipedia article, chosen because it is uniform, cited and reproducible (the revision id is recorded for every one). Claude Sonnet 5 retold the myth in 200 to 300 words using only the article, then rewrote it with every personal, divine, place, people and language-specific name replaced by a role: the hero, the sky god, a great lake, a foreign people, a grain crop. Actions, sequence, objects, animals by kind and numbers were kept. Below, Viracocha before and after.

Does the blinding hold? No. A judge that recognises a story is not blind. Claude Opus 5 was shown every de-identified text and asked to name the region and the figure. It named the region correctly for of stories, said it could not tell for , and was right with high confidence for of the it was confident about. That leak is reported for every target below, and it changes what the lineups measure: not a blind reading of two texts but the model's considered judgment of two stories it knows, with the names removed. The lineups are kept because that judgment is still worth having, with the controls to calibrate it. A second instrument that cannot recognise anything is added in the next section.

04

A judge that knows nothing

Because the language models recognise the stories, the same de-identified texts were also compared by two instruments that cannot: a small local sentence-embedding model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, 22 million parameters, which maps a text to a vector by its wording and cannot name a myth) and plain TF-IDF word overlap. For every pair, the question is where the partner ranks among all other stories in the corpus by cosine similarity to the target: rank 1 means the partner is the single most similar story of the hundred-odd, rank 50 means it is in the middle. These are blunt instruments, sensitive to wording and length, and the positive controls show how blunt.

05

The lineups

One target story, ten candidates, all blind. One candidate is the planted partner; the other nine are random culture-hero stories from regions other than the target's and the partner's. The judge ranks all ten by story content: roles, actions, sequence, what is brought or taught, the specific motifs, weighing an odd shared episode far above "a god, a journey, a punishment". If the planted story is nothing special it ranks first one time in ten and averages 5.5; if it stands out it ranks first most of the time. Each pair is run both ways with ten different decoy draws, by two judges, Claude Opus 5 and Claude Sonnet 5.

Four sets. The civilisers: the pairs the claim rests on, Viracocha with Quetzalcoatl, Oannes and Osiris, Quetzalcoatl with Oannes and Osiris, Oannes with Osiris, and Bochica, Votan, Thoth and Nommo with their supposed counterparts. Documented transmissions, the yardstick: the Gilgamesh flood and the Genesis flood, Atra-Hasis and Gilgamesh, Utnapishtim and Noah, the Ramayana with the Khmer Reamker and the Thai Ramakien, the life of the Buddha and the Christian legend of Barlaam and Josaphat that was made from it, the Panchatantra and its Arabic translation Kalila wa-Dimna, Manu and Matsya, Deucalion and Noah. Related by contact: Quetzalcoatl and Kukulkan, Viracocha and Manco Cápac, Osiris and Isis, Enki and Oannes, Fuxi and Nüwa, the Māori and Hawaiian Māui, Idris and Enoch. Random: a random target and a random partner from another region, the base rate.

09

Limits

Wikipedia is a secondary source. Its articles compress and select, and for the American civilisers they already carry the colonial-era framing (bearded, white, from the sea) that is itself in question. A full study retells from the primary sources: Betanzos, Cieza de León and Sarmiento for Viracocha; Sahagún and the Anales de Cuauhtitlan for Quetzalcoatl; Berossus for Oannes; Plutarch and the Pyramid Texts for Osiris.

The blinding leaks. A large language model has read every mythology. Removing names removes the easy route to recognition, not the hard one, and the leak test shows how often the region could still be named. Where a judge recognises both stories in a pair, its ranking may be its opinion about the pair rather than its reading of two texts. The leak column in the pair table marks those cases.

Two judges from one company. The other accounts were out of credit. Study 3 showed that judges from different companies can disagree a great deal; this page should be re-run with GPT and Gemini before its numbers are trusted.

Story similarity is not transmission. The positive controls show what a known transmission looks like to this instrument. A civiliser pair that matched them would be alike as stories; that is necessary for the claim, not sufficient. A pair that does not match them has not been shown to be unrelated, only to be no more alike than strangers.

What would change the verdict. A civiliser pair picked out first in more than half its lineups by both judges, with a leak test that could not name either story, and at least as often as the documented transmissions.
