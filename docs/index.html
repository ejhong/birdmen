01 / Start with the objects

A resemblance worth taking seriously.

The question belongs to everyone who notices the pattern. No single author owns it.

The most interesting version of this inquiry begins before an explanation: put the actual objects beside one another, look closely, and say exactly what seems alike.

Birdlike profiles. Bent limbs. Rounded forms. Carvings arranged across a standing stone. Some correspondences survive a closer look; others change when we see the whole surface, another photograph or the history of the object.

The central case now includes a third object: the Orongo birdman boulder, a much more direct bird-and-egg comparison. Its museum record and a photograph published in 1919 let us move beyond an uncaptioned image circulating online.

Our task is to distinguish three questions: what is visible, how unusual is the resemblance, and what history would explain it? A score for one cannot answer all three.

02 / Possible histories

Several ways for a form to travel—or recur.

A shared inheritance, contact between peoples, repeated invention, and the effects of selecting and presenting images can all contribute. They make different predictions.

A case for transmission becomes stronger when an unusual combination of features appears along a dated sequence of places, with other evidence of contact. A case for convergence becomes stronger when local predecessors explain the designs and comparable combinations recur widely. Neither explanation earns a free pass.

A proposed deep inheritance

Graham Hancock

In Magicians of the Gods, Hancock proposes that survivors of an Ice Age civilisation carried knowledge to later societies after catastrophe. The appeal is a historical explanation for echoes across monuments, traditions and stories of teachers.

For this inquiry, that becomes a concrete question: can we identify a distinctive inherited design, establish its chronology, and find independent traces of its transmission? The existence of an impressive resemblance would be a starting point; it would not by itself identify that civilisation.

Hancock’s own account of the book · His account of Karahan Tepe. Guest articles on his website have their own authors. This is a summary of his broader proposal, not an attribution of every image comparison to him.

A proposed southern source

Bruce Fenton

Fenton offers a more geographically specific account: a maritime tradition from Island Southeast Asia and northern Australia, with related imagery reaching Göbekli Tepe and other distant places.

His images and full argument remain available in a separate illustrated essay. That record distinguishes what he claims from what the photographs and sources establish. Its most useful next tests concern the identities and dates of the proposed Australian parallels and the missing intermediate examples.

Read the illustrated argument and source checks · Preserved thread transcript. His proposed route is a hypothesis, not a mapped archaeological finding.

These proposals are considered alongside local development and documented regional transmission. The people who made these objects, and living Rapanui knowledge, remain central to their interpretation.

03 / An instrument under examination

First, can the AI see what is there?

That is the first test the earlier image experiments needed.

Some saved responses describe a frontal moai when the photograph shows its carved back. Other responses notice the bird figures. The disagreement affects both high and low similarity scores. We cannot turn an instrument’s failure to read an image into a historical conclusion.

The replacement work starts with recognition, explicit uncertainty, and source-linked observations. Only then should we measure resemblance against carefully chosen alternatives. AI can help assemble and interrogate the evidence; its confidence is not a substitute for it.

The controlled follow-up improves the labels but exposes wrongly located carvings. We can now inspect exactly where a promising score breaks down.

01 · Exploratory shape metric

Indus & rongorongo

When do similar signs become evidence of a connection?

Finding / scope
Under this catalogue metric, the pair ranked near the middle of the comparison set. Rankings changed when font controls were distorted to resemble scanned drawings; the famous chart also differs from the catalogue forms.
Limitations
The combined metric retrieved the correct independent rendition first in about 75% of validation queries, below HOG alone at about 81%. Its results describe this representation and metric; they do not settle perceived resemblance, linguistic affinity or historical contact.

02 · Catalogue experiment

Birds, serpents & sacred centres

Do the familiar motifs travel together?

Finding / scope
In the sampled catalogue, the three broad motif classes are widespread. Their bundle does not stand out against the study's documentation-preserving comparisons.
Limitations
Broad keyword classes are not a test of this bird-and-disc composition. Coverage is uneven, and missing catalogue entries are not evidence of absence. Geographic dispersion alone cannot distinguish invention from transmission.

03 · Historical interpretation suspended

The pillar & the moai

Can AI measure the resemblance people see?

Finding / scope
The saved responses expose perception errors as well as prompt and presentation effects. Opus placed the pair near the top of its distant comparisons; GPT-5.5 ranked many more alternatives above it. Those disagreeing observations remain available.
Limitations
Some judges described a face on the moai’s back or missed its bird figures. The prompts also mixed visible similarity with shared-origin judgments. These pilots cannot establish that the resemblance is ordinary, exceptional or historically explained.

04 · V1 experiment · V2 source audit

The civilisers

Where does the resemblance between teaching figures come from?

Finding / scope
V1's claimed partners ranked first in 62.5% of completed lineups, versus 9% for random pairs. The new V2 audit inspects 29 source passages; it has not rerun the similarity test.
Limitations
The lineups were recognisable and used compressed modern accounts. The new audit is small, purposively selected and unblinded; neither establishes a worldwide rarity rate or a shared origin.

05 · Text-coding pilot

The raven & the dove

What is shared, and what is regional, in flood stories?

Finding / scope
The coded summaries show regional clustering and an uneven distribution of details associated with Genesis, alongside widespread generic flood motifs.
Limitations
Regional structure is compatible with ancient inheritance. Recording after contact does not establish borrowing. One AI coded a compiler's summaries, not a representative collection of original accounts.
