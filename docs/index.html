Start with the objects

A resemblance worth investigating.

First describe what is alike. Then ask how unusual it is, and what history could explain it.

The Orongo birdman boulder makes the central question sharper: a bird-headed figure beside a rounded object. A museum record and a 1919 photograph let us examine the carving behind the circulating image.

Possible histories

Inheritance, contact,
or recurrence?

A dated chain of unusual shared details would strengthen transmission. Local predecessors and widespread equivalents would strengthen convergence. Selection and presentation can also amplify a resemblance.

A proposed deep inheritance

Graham Hancock

Hancock proposes that survivors of an Ice Age civilisation carried knowledge to later societies. The test is to find distinctive inherited forms, establish their chronology and trace their transmission.

His account of Magicians of the Gods. Guest articles on his site have their own authors.

A proposed southern source

Bruce Fenton

Fenton proposes a maritime tradition linking northern Australia and Island Southeast Asia with distant imagery. His specific comparisons need secure identities, dates and intermediate examples.

Illustrated argument and source checks.
