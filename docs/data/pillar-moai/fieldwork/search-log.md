exhaustive search of ancient iconography. Dates below refer to specific kinds of
evidence. No unsuccessful lookup establishes that a motif is absent worldwide.

## Search boundaries and productive next leads

The search followed excavator, museum, author and early documentary references.
It was not a systematic review across all languages, collections or archaeological
reports. The nine dated entries are an evidence trail, not a migration sequence.
In particular, no dated intermediary chain carrying the full bird–limb–rounded-form
arrangement between Anatolia and Rapa Nui was established in this work.

- Follow the paired/manupiri comparisons cited by Pitts et al., including the
  Peabody-held boulder and Horley & Lee's 2012 treatment. Confirm accession,
  photographs, find context and date independently before adding object records.
- Seek the earliest securely documented Orongo bird-and-egg scenes and the strongest
  local carving sequence. Collection dates alone cannot establish their beginnings.
- Expand the Anatolian search under a written site/report boundary, retaining
  bird compositions without discs and discs without birds. Record exposure and
  preservation coverage before making absence claims.
- For the Assyrian anchor, seek a larger source photograph or another documented
  view of the cone. Preserve the museum identification and the present image's
  ambiguity as different evidence statements.
- Investigate intermediate regions with specific relational predictions fixed
  before retrieval. Geographic dots and very broad bird symbolism are insufficient
  to discriminate transmission from independent development.
