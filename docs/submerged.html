What would a map of 12,000 years ago require?

A dated regional sea-level model, the elevation of the former land surface and uncertainty for both. A single global “lower the water” setting cannot recover that history. The Last Glacial Maximum was earlier than 12,000 years ago; their shorelines must not be interchanged.

The North Sea’s published peat records and land-motion corrections are a promising next step. Here, “12,000 years BP” would mean 12,000 years before 1950—approximately 10,050 BCE—not the date of every inundation.

The initial assessment

The missing landscape is real.
Its contents are a research question.

Submerged archaeological sites and drowned land surfaces are documented. They give us good reasons to search beyond modern coastlines. They do not establish that most important evidence—or a particular lost civilisation—must be there. Pavlopetri Underwater Archaeology Project: research impact record · Aboriginal artefacts on the continental shelf reveal ancient drowned cultural landscapes in northwest Australia

The productive question is where past habitability, preservation and usable survey data overlap. That turns a vast mystery into investigations we can actually attempt.

From an idea to a research programme

Start with one defensible landscape.

Our initial choice: the North Sea. This is an editorial assessment of feasibility, based on the sources above.

  1. Reconstruct before searching.

    Reproduce one dated regional shoreline with uncertainty, using the published sea-level data and an appropriate former land surface. Compare it explicitly with the modern-depth shortcut.

  2. Find where the data are strongest.

    Map survey footprints, resolution, interpolation and sediment cover. Identify gaps before interpreting a blank map as archaeological absence.

  3. Ask a small, testable question.

    For example: which mapped former freshwater margins combine a plausible occupation window, preserved sediments and accessible detailed survey data?

  4. Make every candidate answerable.

    Use independent surveys and geological alternatives to check proposed features. AI can help catalogue patterns; validation must distinguish natural landforms, survey artefacts and human activity.

Follow the evidence

Sources for this assessment.

No archaeological discoveries or dated shorelines were inferred from this demonstrator. Records checked 2026-09-19.

  1. active project

    SUBNORDICA: beyond submerged landscapes

    Official project period: April 2024–March 2030. Uses North Sea and Baltic data to develop methods for identifying archaeological targets.

  2. research paper

    Global sea-level rise in the early Holocene revealed from North Sea peats

    Hijma et al., 2025. Dated peat sequences, regional land-motion corrections and uncertainty; a route toward a defensible regional reconstruction.

  3. open data

    North Sea peats: supplementary information and data

    Repository API checked: CC BY 4.0. Contains the HOLSEA spreadsheet, age-calibration scripts, core photographs, technical documentation and model grids. Files listed; scientific contents not yet independently reanalysed.

  4. proposing researchers’ abstract

    Major marine archaeological discovery in the Gulf of Cambay and its significance

    Kathiroli and NIOT colleagues, 2004 conference abstract. Reports sonar patterns, palaeochannel-like features and grab/dredge samples. The source proposes structures; it is not an independent confirmation of that interpretation.

  5. institutional research record

    Pavlopetri Underwater Archaeology Project: research impact record

    Documents the project begun in 2009, mapped buildings and ceramics indicating occupation from about 3500 BCE through the Bronze Age. Historical project record; current fieldwork not verified.

  6. field report

    Pavlopetri: 2010 field report

    Reports structural survey, sector-scan sonar and stereo photogrammetry. A useful example of multiple recording methods applied to the same identified site.

  7. research paper

    Aboriginal artefacts on the continental shelf reveal ancient drowned cultural landscapes in northwest Australia

    Benjamin et al., 2020. Reports Cape Bruguieres and Flying Foam Passage, combining remote sensing and diver inspection. Site-formation and sea-level reasoning must accompany the artefact claims.

  8. research paper / indexed abstract

    A submerged Stone Age hunting architecture from the Western Baltic Sea

    Geersen et al., 2024; DOI 10.1073/pnas.2312008121. Hydroacoustic, sediment and optical observations support an anthropogenic wall interpretation. Hunting use and age are contextual inferences.

  9. research paper

    Prehistoric human migration between Sundaland and South Asia was driven by sea-level rise

    2023 study combining sea-level and population models. The reconstructed landscape is relevant to targeting; it is not a discovery of a submerged city.

  10. research paper

    Sea level and global ice volumes from the Last Glacial Maximum to the Holocene

    Lambeck et al., 2014. Global ice-volume and sea-level history distinguishes the glacial maximum from later deglaciation and regional relative sea level.

  11. dataset documentation

    GEBCO_2025 Grid and Type Identifier grid

    Versioned global 15 arc-second elevation compilation. The TID grid identifies input-data types; spatial grid spacing must not be treated as uniform survey accuracy.

  12. dataset documentation

    EMODnet Bathymetry DTM 2024 release

    Release announcement dated 24 March 2025. European grid at 1/16 arc-minute spacing, with multiple contributing survey methods.

  13. data service

    EMODnet DTM 2024: ERDDAP data access

    Metadata endpoint checked. Variables include elevation, source index, count and interpolation flag; these help audit provenance rather than treating every pixel as measured.

  14. data service

    NOAA ETOPO1: versioned ERDDAP relief dataset

    The demonstrator uses small subsamples of this older 2009 global relief model. Four actual NetCDF downloads and hashes are retained. Mean-sea-level elevations; not an ancient coastline model.

  15. data archive

    NCEI Marine Trackline Geophysical Data

    Searchable bathymetry, sub-bottom, side-scan, magnetic, gravity and seismic records. Coverage and acquisition quality vary by survey; some files require additional handling.

  16. method documentation

    ESA: satellite-derived bathymetry

    Optical shallow-water method sensitive to clarity, clouds, glint and bottom properties. Different from inferring broad seafloor relief through satellite altimetry.

  17. research record

    Space-borne bathymetry with ICESat-2 and Sentinel-2

    NASA-hosted 2021 study describes calibration and validation of shallow, clear-water bathymetry. No universal depth limit or archaeological-detection capability is implied.

  18. data access portal

    Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem

    Portal for Sentinel observations. This edition links acquisition access; no satellite scene has yet been processed for this programme.
