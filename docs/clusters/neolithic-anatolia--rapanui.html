Line 01 · Evidence examined

Birdmen across two worlds

Birds, bent limbs and rounded forms, across a vast interval in place and time.

↔
What appears to correspond
  • Both groups include bird imagery in complex carved or painted scenes.
  • The narrow bird-and-rounded-form correspondence is strongest for Pillar 43 and the Orongo boulder; it is not equally present in every group member.
What differs or remains uncertain
  • The Rapanui group includes human–bird hybrids; the Pillar 43 figure is generally described as a vulture.
  • The moai’s ring is below the birdmen; the boulder’s rounded object is at a bent hand; Pillar 43’s disc lies above a wing.
  • The group samples were selected for this inquiry. They do not establish how common or rare the combination is.

Line 02 · Sources partly checked

Hands drawn toward the body

Anatolian, Pacific and Andean figures invite a comparison of gesture.

↔
What appears to correspond
  • Arms descend beside a frontal body; the hands converge or act near the torso.
What differs or remains uncertain
  • The Ponce figure holds objects; the other poses need separate descriptions.
  • The hands are not necessarily directed at the same anatomical point.
  • Similar human bodies constrain the number of comfortable or legible sculptural poses.

Line 03 · Sources partly checked

Bodies with visible ribs

Karahantepe’s stone figure and the moai kavakava repertoire.

↔
What appears to correspond
  • Both selected examples emphasize ribs and the human body.
What differs or remains uncertain
  • The stone statue is monumental; the wooden figures are small and portable.
  • The official discovery account calls the Karahantepe figure seated.
  • The circulating historical photograph’s “1730” identifier should not become a collection date.

Keep in the frame

What would move this cluster forward?

Collected comparisons accumulate; they do not compound into proof. Each line still needs its own dated sequence.

  • Build dated local sequences on both sides.
  • Use expert-reviewed feature locations and relations before any similarity ranking.
  • Add a documented comparison corpus selected independently of the proposed match.
  • Trace object dates and acquisition identities.
  • Code exact hand positions, held objects and local sculptural predecessors.
  • Identify the exact photographed wooden figure.
  • Compare local variants and funerary interpretations before inferring shared meaning.

Sources & scope

Provisional editorial records with no independent specialist sign-off. Review stages describe how far sources were checked, not the probability of a historical connection.

    Excavation-team discussion of the depicted animals and competing readings of the disc and upper forms.

  3. museum · British Museum

    Identifies the statue and back carvings; construction and later carving episodes must be separated.

  4. museum · British Museum / Google Arts & Culture

    Orongo provenance, birdman with rounded object, and an approximate 1750–1875 catalogue date.

  5. excavation · Jens Notroff / DAI

    Dances with cranes: animal masquerade

    Discusses birds and possible human-like legs on regional pillars. Interpretation as masquerade remains a proposal.

  6. museum · Museo Antropológico Padre Sebastián Englert

    Painted slabs from Orongo · MAPSE 17-00-1312

    Two facing tangata manu on a house slab; the 1974 move to the museum does not date the painting.

  7. claim · Lee Paqui; hosted by Graham Hancock

    Karahan Tepe’s figure and the moai kavakava

    Guest article, 19 October 2024. Proposes ribbed-figure and bird-with-rounded-object parallels. Its medical and ancestral interpretations are not established by the photographs.

  8. primary-text · Katherine Routledge

    The Mystery of Easter Island (1919)

    Figures 110 and 112 preserve photographs of the Orongo rock and birdman boulder. Publication dates are not carving dates.

  9. claim · Hugh Newman; hosted by Graham Hancock

    Megalithic origins: Göbekli Tepe and Peru

    Guest article, 29 March 2014. Specific comparisons include hands toward the abdomen, sacred centres and distant statuary.

  10. project · Deep Memory

    The project’s illustrated comparison archive

    Records comparisons assembled from this repository and supplied material. An external first proposer has not been traced for every pair.

  11. excavation · Türkiye Ministry of Culture and Tourism

    2023 Karahantepe and Göbeklitepe discoveries

    30 September 2023: 2.3 m human statue with emphasized ribs and spine, described as seated; the article’s “standing” label needs care.

  12. museum · British Museum

    Moai kavakava · Oc,+.2595

    A documented example of the type, late eighteenth to mid-nineteenth century. Not an established identification of the figure in the catalogue’s historical photograph.

  13. image-record · British Museum photographic collection / Wikimedia Commons

    Historic front and side views · Oc,G.T.1730

    The linked image identifier contains 1730; it is not a demonstrated collection year. Individual sculpture identity and age remain to be tied to a collection record.
