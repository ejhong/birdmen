Teachers from another world

Oannes, Viracocha and Quetzalcoatl, compared at the level of passages.

What appears to correspond
  • Teaching or institutions are associated with each figure.
  • Water or sea departures occur in different parts of the selected accounts.
What differs or remains uncertain
  • Arrival from the sea, nightly return to the sea and final departure across it are different events.
  • Recording dates are not the dates at which the traditions began.
  • The compared accounts do not establish one matching promise of personal return.

Sources & scope

Provisional editorial records with no independent specialist sign-off. Review stages describe how far sources were checked, not the probability of a historical connection.

  1. project · Deep Memory

    Teaching figures: primary-source audit

    29 inspected passages. The sea-arrival, teaching, departure and promised-return motifs do not all occur together in the compared accounts.

  2. primary-text · Berossus / Eusebius

    Berossus through Eusebius, Chronicle I

    Oannes appears from the sea, teaches and returns to it at night. Berossus’s work survives through later witnesses and translation.

  3. primary-text · Pedro Sarmiento de Gamboa

    History of the Incas (1572)

    Colonial witness for Viracocha; the recording date is not the origin date of the tradition.

  4. primary-text · Bernardino de Sahagún and Nahua collaborators

    Historia general / Florentine Codex

    Sixteenth-century witness. See the project’s Q1–Q4 locators for teachings and departure, with translation qualifications.

  5. claim · Graham Hancock

    Fingerprints of the Gods: author’s account

    Primary source for Hancock’s broad proposed relationship between Old and New World monuments and traditions, not a source for each guest author’s claim.
