01 / A controlled follow-up

Change one factor at a time.

The first diagnostic confirmed that the model sometimes misses birds or invents a face on the moai’s back. This follow-up crosses one versus two photographs with low versus high reasoning effort, using the same GPT-5.5 snapshot in every condition.

Each of the three objects has photographs A and B. One request starts with A, the other with B. The paired condition adds the alternate view. The model always describes and locates features in the first image. All 24 requests are fresh; their order, prompt, image bytes and reference answers were fixed before the run.

We separately count the checks that matter most: birds on all three surfaces, no large human face on the moai’s back, and a scorpion on the pillar. That gives ten primary answers per condition. All five features give thirty answers per condition.

Three objects, six known photographs; no held-out sample. More images also add image tokens. Two input orders are not repeated identical trials. Reference answers are provisional and have no independent expert review. Full protocol.

03 / Look again

“Scorpion present.”
But the box points to a bird.

Every Pillar 43 answer that said “scorpion present” placed its box on the upper panel. The excavation team identifies the scorpion on the lower shaft. A correct categorical answer can therefore conceal an incorrect reading.

This is a post-run visual audit of all three positive scorpion answers, performed by the implementing AI with the source description and photographs. The approximate source guides are not specialist annotations. The original scores remain unchanged; the audit adds a reason not to treat them as verified perception accuracy.

05 / What this changes

Seeing needs its own evidence.

An AI can score a resemblance while misreading the very things being compared.

In this small development set, higher reasoning improved the categorical answers in both view conditions. Adding a second view helped some answers and harmed others. Even the strongest label result still included uncertainty about the moai’s face and wrongly located the pillar’s scorpion.

The next historical comparison therefore needs verified locations and relationships, not just presence labels. Independent reviewers should check whether a box covers the named carving, whether a limb really meets a rounded object, and whether the same reading survives another photograph.

That work can sharpen the resemblance. It cannot by itself identify a lost civilisation, prove independent invention, or supply the missing history between Anatolia and Rapa Nui. The local evidence and our working explanation remain separate from this test of the instrument.
