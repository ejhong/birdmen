# A catalogue of claimed anomalous similarities

Design notes from the 19 September 2026 discussion. This is the proposed next
direction, not a completed worldwide search or implemented application.

## Purpose

Build a broad, eventually comprehensive catalogue of similarities people have
pointed out across artefacts, monuments, imagery, myths and recorded traditions,
focusing on cases for which ordinary diffusion does not readily account.

The central browsing collection is **claimed anomalies**. Preserve the strongest
specific version of each claim and the reason it is puzzling. Do not require a
favoured historical explanation or treat the claim of anomaly as already proven.
Known transmission and local parallels are useful linked controls, available as
an optional layer. They should not displace the core collection.

Breadth and verification are separate dimensions. A sourced lead can enter before
all its questions are resolved. Corrections, disputed identifications and modern
reproductions stay accessible with their history; they must not recur as fresh
discoveries after their original correction is forgotten.

## What to catalogue separately

| Record | Why it needs its own identity |
| --- | --- |
| Physical object / archaeological surface | One object may have many photographs, decorated surfaces and carving episodes. |
| Tradition / account / passage | A story, its earliest surviving witness, a translation and a modern paraphrase are different evidence. |
| Image / recording / illustration | Photography, casts, reconstructions, tracings and generated illustrations can change a comparison. |
| Comparison claim | Who compared which features of which objects or accounts, where, and with what proposed explanation? |
| Source | Collection record, excavation report, primary account, research paper or popular claim source, with exact locators. |
| Review / revision | Who checked an identification, image, date or interpretation; what changed and what remains disputed? |

A comparison can involve more than two examples. The database must retain its
membership without converting every member into supposedly independent evidence.
Motif tags connect records; they do not themselves establish a common origin.

## Claim dossier

Lead with the actual comparison, using verified photographs or source passages
where available, then explain:

1. **The claim:** its author/source, specific matched features and strongest version.
2. **Why it seems anomalous:** distance, date gap, proposed isolation, unusual
   combination, or mismatch with an established transmission history.
3. **What the evidence is:** object identities, image transformations, source
   passages, dated episodes and the basis of those dates.
4. **What matches and differs:** entity, outline, posture, relationship, layout,
   sequence and meaning as separate observations. Preserve uncertainty.
5. **Diffusion check:** documented transmission, plausible but unproven route,
   contested account, or no established route in the sources reviewed. Absence
   of a found route is not proof that contact never occurred.
6. **Local and comparative controls:** link variants and documented transmission
   cases relevant to this particular claim.
7. **What would change the reading:** concrete missing identification, earlier
   attestation, intermediate object, improved photograph or independent review.

Keep source verification, object/image authenticity, dating confidence and the
historical inference separate. A real ancient object can appear in a misleading
montage. A faithful image does not establish a connection. Avoid a single opaque
“anomaly score”, especially one based on unvalidated AI recognition.

## Browsing and visualisation

**Visual gallery (default).** Comparison cards show the actual pairs or small
groups, a one-sentence anomaly claim, locations, date ranges and source status.
Filter by motif, place, period, medium, kind of evidence and diffusion status.
Opening a card keeps the current filters and opens its full dossier.

**Map and timeline (linked).** Show original contexts or findspots; current museum
locations are a separate choice. Map lines mean “a proposed comparison”, with an
explicit legend, not voyages or migration. Documented transmission uses a distinct
relationship type. Default to the selected claim or cluster, with controls optional.
Use date bands and uncertain boundaries; never silently replace broad ranges
with exact midpoint dates. Filters must specify whether they include any interval
overlap or only wholly contained ranges. Unknown dates remain browseable.

Stories require dates of recording, earliest surviving witness, and proposed
earlier composition/origin as separate fields. Date sources and certainty must
travel with every value. Map regional traditions as regions when a precise
location is unjustified. Separate “recorded here” from “originated here”.

**Motif collections.** Examples include bird-and-rounded-object compositions,
figures holding animals, unusual hand positions, flood birds and arriving teachers.
Allow broad family tags and narrower relational tags. A general bird motif must
not automatically count as a match for a bird holding a disc or a particular
sequence of released birds in a story.

**Focused graph.** Start with one object, tradition, motif or claim and expand its
neighbours. Distinguish claims, evidence and controls. Avoid a default worldwide
tangle or layout proximity that looks like a measure of historical affinity.

**Guided essays.** Curate the best documented clusters into readable, illustrated
investigations, drawing from the same underlying records. Keep the pillar–moai–
boulder case as the first deep example. The catalogue and essays should strengthen
each other, not require duplicate maintenance.

Use shareable URLs for selections and filters. On mobile, the gallery and dossier
take priority; map and timeline remain optional views. Provide readable lists and
tables alongside graphics, keyboard navigation and downloadable source records.

## Acquisition and growth

Begin by converting the existing repo’s comparison material into explicit claims,
including unresolved supplied images. Then search broadly across books, research,
museum records, videos and public discussions to discover additional proposed
anomalies. Capture the proposing source before replacing weak imagery with better
photographs; the original presentation itself may need auditing.

Verification follows collection records and primary sources where possible.
Record unsuccessful source searches and unresolved identifications. Deduplicate
objects, image derivatives, reused montages and accounts dependent on the same
source. Do not confuse many websites repeating a claim with independent support.

AI can assist discovery, extraction, translation, candidate identity matches and
tag suggestions. Each proposed factual identification needs evidence. Preserve
model uncertainty and reviewer disagreements; require independently checked
locations and relations before automated visual judgments support conclusions.

Build one complete vertical slice first: a claim gallery, usable map/timeline,
and full source dossier over a modest reviewed set. Grow discovery coverage while
tracking verification depth. The eventual catalogue can be comprehensive without
pretending the first edition is.
